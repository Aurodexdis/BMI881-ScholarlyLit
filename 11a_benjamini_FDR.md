## *Controlling the False Discovery Rate: a Practical and Powerful Approach to Multiple Testing* $-$ Benjamini & Hochberg

### Summary:
Compared to the conventional familywise error rate (FWER), this research paper presents a novel method for managing the False Discovery Rate (FDR) in multiple hypothesis testing. The authors show that traditional multiple comparison procedures (MCPs) that regulate FWER are frequently neglected and overly conservative in applied research. To control the percentage of incorrectly rejected hypotheses among all rejected hypotheses, they suggest a sequential Bonferroni-style approach. They demonstrate through simulations and theoretical arguments that their methodology provides far greater statistical power than conventional methods while preserving significant error control, particularly in cases where several null hypotheses are incorrect.

### Reaction:
This work was intriguing as it thoroughly questioned the then-dominant paradigm of FWER control in multiple testing. In many real-world situations, researchers are ready to accept a small number of false positives if they can identify more true positives. The authors' acknowledgment of this was quite progressive. The way they illustrated when FDR control is preferable compared to FWER control using real-world examples from clinical trials was really nice to see. This work is especially appealing because of the mathematical elegance of their process and its usefulness.

### Discussion Questions:
1. What are the possible consequences of researchers accepting false discoveries too readily, and how has the implementation of FDR control altered the field of multiple testing in our respective areas of study?
2. According to the authors, when assessing numerous endpoints in clinical trials, FDR control is preferable to FWER control. What moral ramifications does this method have for medical research?
3. When working with dependent test statistics, how could the Benjamini-Hochberg process be altered, and what are the real-world effects of doing so?
